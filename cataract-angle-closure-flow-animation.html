When the drainage angle closes

Aqueous fluid normally flows behind the iris, through the pupil, and into the anterior chamber. As the lens clouds and swells (a cataract), it thickens and pushes toward the iris. Once the lens touches the iris, the iris bows forward against the cornea, closing the drainage angle. Fluid is now trapped — circling between the zonules, lens, and iris instead of draining out.
