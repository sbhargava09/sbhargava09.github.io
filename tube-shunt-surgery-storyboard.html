Concept preview — storyboard, not yet animated

Glaucoma tube shunt (Ahmed valve) surgery

Five key stills mapping out the sequence, drawn on the same eye/eyelid coordinate system as the aqueous drainage animation so it can be tweened afterward. Review these, then we'll turn it into a timed animation.

Frame 1  ·  Baseline

Primary gaze, pre-op. Eye at rest, normal lid aperture — matches the starting pose of the aqueous drainage animation.

Frame 2  ·  Exposure

Eye rotated down, lids held wide. A speculum and an inferior traction suture bring the superior sclera into view for the conjunctival peritomy.

Frame 3  ·  Flap opened

Conjunctival flap raised. The conjunctiva (and Tenon's) is incised on three sides and reflected forward over the hinge at the limbus — here laid temporarily over the cornea — exposing bare sclera for the plate.

Frame 4  ·  Plate placed

Plate sutured, tube inserted. The plate is long and narrow, slid back along the same meridian into a sub-Tenon's pocket well posterior to the limbus; the tube runs forward from its anterior tip and crosses the limbus, its visible segment reaching about halfway from the limbus to the pupil.

Frame 5  ·  Flap closed

Flap replaced, closure in progress. The same conjunctival flap is folded back down over the plate and patch graft. A few interrupted sutures are already in, and one more is being placed at the margin before the eye is allowed to settle.

Frame 6  ·  Final result

Back to primary gaze. Lids return to their resting position. Only a short stub of tube is visible crossing the superotemporal limbus — the plate itself sits far enough posterior to be covered by the upper eyelid.

Once these read correctly, the plan is to tween frame 1→2 (downgaze + lid retraction), 2→3 (flap incised & reflected), 3→4 (plate/tube fade in), 4→5 (flap folds back down + suture animates in), and 5→6 (eye recentres, lids relax) — the same blink/lid-tween machinery already used in the aqueous drainage animation.
