Phacomorphic angle closure

As a cataractous lens swells and opacifies (phacomorphic change), it thickens axially and pushes forward, meeting the iris at the pupil margin. Once apposed, aqueous can no longer pass forward easily, so pressure bows the iris (iris bombe) and drives its root peripherally until it covers the trabecular meshwork — closing the eye's drainage angle. Proportions follow the same biometry as the static diagram; the pace of the animation is illustrative, not a real-time timeline.
