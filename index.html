About

A physician who believes patients deserve clarity.

“I want every patient to leave their appointment better informed about their eyes than when they arrived.”

Dr. Siddharth Bhargava is a fellowship-trained ophthalmologist with specialized expertise in glaucoma management, cataract surgery, premium intraocular lens counseling, and complex anterior segment care.

He completed his medical degree at Albany Medical College and his ophthalmology residency at Eastern Virginia Medical School, followed by a glaucoma fellowship at Washington University in St. Louis — where he focused on medical and surgical glaucoma, micro-invasive glaucoma surgery (MIGS), and the intersection of glaucoma and lens-based surgery.

His approach to care is built on patient education: helping people understand not just what treatment is recommended, but why — and what to expect at every step. Dr. Bhargava speaks English, Hindi, and Urdu and welcomes patients from diverse backgrounds.

Frequently Asked Questions

Questions patients often ask

A first visit typically includes a thorough history, vision testing, intraocular pressure measurements, slit-lamp examination, and imaging or visual field testing when indicated. The goal is to understand your eye health fully and discuss a clear, reasoned plan together.

Timing depends on how your cataract is affecting daily activities — reading, driving, glare at night — along with your exam findings and any other eye conditions. We will review potential benefits, risks, and alternatives before making any plan together.

Glaucoma is a long-term condition that can progress silently before affecting central vision. Repeating visual field tests, OCT scans, and pressure checks helps us determine whether the disease is stable or changing — and whether treatment needs to be adjusted.

Micro-invasive glaucoma surgery (MIGS) uses smaller incisions and typically has a faster recovery and a more favorable safety profile than traditional procedures such as trabeculectomy or tube shunts. MIGS is often performed alongside cataract surgery. The right approach depends on the severity of your glaucoma and individual factors.
