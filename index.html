About

About Dr. Bhargava

Dr. Siddharth Bhargava is a fellowship-trained ophthalmologist with specialized expertise in glaucoma management, cataract surgery, premium intraocular lens counseling, and complex anterior segment care. He completed his medical degree at Albany Medical College and his ophthalmology residency at Eastern Virginia Medical School, followed by a glaucoma fellowship at Washington University in St. Louis.

His clinical interests include medical and surgical glaucoma, micro-invasive glaucoma surgery (MIGS), cataract surgery, and advanced anterior segment procedures. He is committed to helping every patient better understand their condition and feel confident in their care plan.

Dr. Bhargava speaks English, Hindi, and Urdu and welcomes patients from diverse backgrounds. He is an active member of the American Glaucoma Society and the American Society of Cataract and Refractive Surgery.

Clinical expertise

Areas of focus

Comprehensive ophthalmology

Evaluation and ongoing care for a wide range of eye conditions, including routine concerns, medical eye disease, and long-term monitoring for changes over time.

Glaucoma care

Diagnosis, monitoring, and treatment of glaucoma with a focus on long-term vision preservation. Treatment may include medications, laser procedures, MIGS, or traditional glaucoma surgeries when appropriate.

Cataract surgery

Evaluation and counseling on cataract surgery timing, what to expect from the procedure, and how surgery may interact with glaucoma or other existing eye conditions.

Premium IOL counseling

In-depth conversations about intraocular lens options based on each patient’s lifestyle, visual priorities, and underlying eye health—including an honest discussion of tradeoffs and realistic expectations.

Complex anterior segment care

Management of advanced or complex conditions involving the front of the eye, including combined glaucoma and cataract procedures and lens-related surgical problem solving.

Collaborative & coordinated care

Close coordination with optometrists, primary care providers, and specialists to support patients with complex medical or ocular histories.

Questions patients often ask

A first visit typically includes a thorough history, vision testing, intraocular pressure measurements, slit-lamp examination, and imaging or visual field testing when indicated. The goal is to understand your eye health and discuss a clear next-steps plan together.

Timing is based on how your cataract is affecting daily activities such as reading, driving, or glare at night, along with your exam findings and any other eye conditions. We will discuss what you are noticing and review potential benefits, risks, and alternatives before making any plan.

Glaucoma is a long-term condition that can progress silently before affecting central vision. Repeating visual field tests, OCT scans, and pressure checks over time helps us determine whether the disease is stable or changing, and whether treatment needs to be adjusted.

Micro-invasive glaucoma surgery (MIGS) refers to a group of newer procedures designed to lower eye pressure with a smaller incision, faster recovery, and a more favorable safety profile than traditional surgeries such as trabeculectomy or tube shunts. MIGS procedures are often performed at the time of cataract surgery. Whether MIGS or a traditional procedure is more appropriate depends on the severity of glaucoma and individual patient factors.
